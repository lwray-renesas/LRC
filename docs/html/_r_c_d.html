Residual Current Devices (RCDs)

This page introduces the key concepts behind Residual Current Devices (RCDs) — sometimes referred to as Residual Current Circuit Breakers (RCCBs)** or Residual Current Devices with Over‑current protection (RCBOs).

The purpose of this material is to establish a conceptual and system‑level understanding of RCD operation, measurement techniques, and regulatory requirements.
Armed with these concepts, the reader should be able to explore detailed standards and product implementations independently.

Principle of Operation

A Residual Current Device protects against electric shock and fire hazards by detecting imbalance between conductors supplying a load.

Under normal operating conditions:

\[ I_\mathrm{L} = I_\mathrm{N} \]

Where:

  • \( I_\mathrm{L} \) is the line (phase) current
  • \( I_\mathrm{N} \) is the neutral current

If some current leaks to earth (e.g. through a human body or faulty insulation):

\[ I_\Delta = | I_\mathrm{L} - I_\mathrm{N} | \]

When this residual current exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g. 30 mA), the RCD disconnects the supply within a specified time.

Sensing Current Difference

Residual current detection is fundamentally based on measuring the vector sum of all live conductors supplying a load.

This is typically achieved using a core‑balance current transformer (CBCT).

Core‑Balance Current Transformer

A core‑balance CT consists of a magnetic core through which all live conductors (L, N, and optional phases) pass together.

  • Under normal operation, the magnetic flux cancels:

    \[ \sum I = 0 \]

  • Under fault conditions, a net flux is produced:

    \[ \sum I \neq 0 \]

This net flux induces a current in the secondary winding proportional to the residual current.

Signal Conditioning

The output of the residual current transformer must be conditioned before it can be processed by analog comparators or ADC subsystems.

Typical signal conditioning stages include:

  • Burden resistor (current‑to‑voltage conversion)
  • Rectification or absolute‑value detection
  • Filtering to suppress high‑frequency noise
  • Gain scaling to match ADC/comparator input range
Warning
Filter time constants directly impact trip time and must be carefully selected to meet regulatory disconnection requirements.

Rogowski-Based Residual Current Sensing

In addition to traditional core‑balance current transformers, Rogowski‑based sensing is increasingly used in modern RCD designs, particularly where wide bandwidth, DC immunity**, or compact mechanical integration is required.

When paired with a suitable analog front end (AFE) such as the RAA788000 and a flux concentrator, Rogowski sensing can be adapted effectively for residual current detection.

Rogowski Principle Applied to RCDs

A Rogowski coil is an air‑cored, toroidal winding that produces a voltage proportional to the time derivative of the enclosed current:

\[ V_\mathrm{coil}(t) = K \frac{dI(t)}{dt} \]

Where:

  • \( I(t) \) is the residual current waveform
  • \( K \) is the mutual inductance constant of the coil

Unlike current transformers:

  • There is no magnetic core saturation
  • Sensitivity is independent of absolute line current
  • Response extends to very low and very high frequencies

For RCD applications, the Rogowski coil is arranged such that all live conductors pass through the sensing aperture, or flux concentrator, measuring only the residual current.

Remarks
Because a Rogowski coil responds to \( dI/dt \), signal integration is required to reconstruct the residual current magnitude.

Flux Concentrator

A key challenge in Rogowski‑based RCD sensing is achieving sufficient sensitivity at low residual current levels (e.g. 10–30 mA).

A flux concentrator addresses this by:

  • Enhancing the magnetic field coupling into the Rogowski coil
  • Improving signal‑to‑noise ratio at low currents
  • Reducing dependency on precise conductor positioning
Warning
Flux concentrators must be carefully designed to avoid introducing hysteresis**, thermal drift, or directional sensitivity that could impact trip consistency.

Rogowski AFE Using RAA788000

The RAA788000 is the first of its kind coil-on-chip rogowski IC with integrated op-amps.

Signal chain:

  1. Rogowski coil output (dI/dt signal) - provided by RAA788000
  2. Low‑noise amplification & filtering - provided by RAA788000
  3. Post processing (MCU + ADC/Comparator)

Key functional attributes include:

  • Low input‑referred noise to support low \( I_\Delta \)
  • Stable integration over temperature and time
  • Predictable phase response for time‑based trip evaluation
Remarks
Using an integrated AFE reduces component tolerance errors and simplifies compliance with trip‑time accuracy requirements.

Trip Logic and Decision Making

Once a residual current signal is obtained, the decision logic determines when to trip.

Two common implementation approaches are used:

Analog Trip

  • Comparator detects residual current above threshold
  • Directly drives trip solenoid or latch
  • Simple, fast, and robust

Digital Trip

  • Residual signal sampled by ADC
  • Digital filtering and integration applied
  • Software determines trip condition
  • Enables:
    • Multiple trip curves
    • Self‑test
    • Event logging
    • Adaptation to different RCD types
Remarks
Digital RCD implementations must carefully manage latency and failure modes to ensure safety equivalence with analog designs.

RCD Types

Standards classify RCD behaviour according to the residual current waveform they can detect.

Type Detects Typical Applications
Type AC Sinusoidal AC only Legacy residential systems
Type A AC + pulsating DC Appliances with rectifiers
Type F AC + pulsating DC + mixed-frequency Variable‑speed drives
Type B AC + DC + smooth DC EV charging, PV inverters

Compliance

RCDs are safety‑critical devices and are subject to stringent regulatory requirements. Compliance ensures correct operation under fault conditions, environmental stress, and electromagnetic disturbance.

Warning
This section provides reference information only. Formal compliance requires consultation with notified bodies and accredited test facilities.

Global Standards – IEC

The International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) defines the foundational standards for RCDs:

Standard Description
IEC 61008 General requirements for RCDs without over‑current protection (RCCB - Residual Current Circuit Breaker)
IEC 61009 General requirements for RCDs with over‑current protection (RCBO- Residual Current Breaker + Overcurrent)
IEC 60947‑2 Industrial circuit breakers with residual current protection
IEC 60755 General safety requirements & testing methods for residual current devices
IEC 62423 Safety, performance & testing requirements for Type F and Type B RCD requirements

Regional / Bloc Standards (EU & UK)

In the EU and UK, IEC standards are adopted through EN standards and enforced via national regulations.

Typical compliance hierarchy:

Step Entity Purpose
1 Requirements (Low Voltage Directive / UK Electrical Safety Regulations) Legal safety requirements
2 Standards (EN 61008, EN 61009, EN 62423) Technical compliance definition
3 Type Testing Functional and safety verification
4 Certification Issuance by notified body
5 Declaration of Conformity Manufacturer declaration

Trip Characteristics

RCD standards define:

  • Rated residual current \( I_{\Delta n} \)
  • Maximum disconnection time as a function of \( I_\Delta \)

Typical values:

IΔn Maximum Trip Time
1 × IΔn ≤ 300 ms
2 × IΔn ≤ 150 ms
5 × IΔn ≤ 40 ms
